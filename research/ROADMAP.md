# HealthPod Research Roadmap

## Phase 0 — Evidence map

Build a living evidence matrix covering aerosol-generation mechanisms, respiratory deposition, aerosol optics, carrier chemistry, material contamination and toxicology.

**Exit criterion:** enough evidence to define benchmark methods and uncertainties without assuming a preferred formulation.

## Phase 1 — Aerosol physics benchmark

Compare candidate mechanisms without optimizing a human-inhalation formulation.

Measure or define methods for:
- emitted aerosol mass;
- particle/droplet size distribution;
- plume optical signal;
- plume persistence and geometry;
- aerosol temperature;
- device energy use;
- reproducibility.

Investigate **Optical Aerosol Efficiency (OAE)** as an exploratory engineering quantity. Do not treat it as a health endpoint.

**Lead candidate:** SAW.

## Phase 2 — Respiratory exposure model

Connect aerosol physics to exposure rather than assuming that a larger cloud equals a larger deposited dose.

Questions:
- What fraction is inhalable?
- Where would different size distributions deposit?
- How should emitted mass, inhaled mass and deposited mass be distinguished?
- Can visible plume performance be partially decoupled from deep-lung deposition?

Use validated respiratory-aerosol models and experimental methods where feasible.

## Phase 3 — Chemistry and materials

For surviving mechanisms, characterize relevant chemical/material emissions.

Candidate endpoint classes:
- carbonyls;
- VOCs;
- metals;
- device-material transfer;
- composition changes caused by aerosolization.

Formulation screening begins only here and is treated as a toxicology problem first, a flavor problem later.

## Phase 4 — Preclinical biology

Candidates that pass physics/exposure/chemistry gates may proceed to appropriate respiratory in-vitro models.

Potential endpoint classes:
- viability;
- oxidative stress;
- inflammatory response;
- epithelial/barrier effects.

## Phase 5 — Sensory research

Only after earlier gates, investigate how aerosol physics relates to perceived plume, mouth/throat sensation and ritual.

No candidate advances to human inhalation merely because it performs well optically.

## Phase 6 — Human research

Not part of the initial build. Any future human inhalation study requires adequate prior evidence, appropriate expert oversight and ethical/regulatory review.

## Next research sprint — formulation

After the mechanism evidence map, investigate candidate carrier/formulation classes by inhalation evidence, aerosolizability, hygroscopic behavior, thermal/acoustic stability, respiratory clearance and known toxicology.

The objective is **not** to invent a recipe. It is to eliminate poorly justified substances and identify the smallest scientifically defensible formulation space for controlled bench testing.

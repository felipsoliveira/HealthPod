# Aerosol Technology Candidates

## Candidate 1 — Surface Acoustic Wave (SAW) atomization

**Priority: highest for the first research cycle.**

SAW uses acoustic energy propagating across a piezoelectric surface to disrupt a liquid film into droplets. Pulmonary-delivery literature demonstrates micron-scale aerosol generation, low-power operation, miniaturization potential and, in some configurations, dense aerosols.

Why HealthPod cares:
- avoids conventional resistive-coil evaporation;
- droplet formation can be studied through fluid mechanics rather than only thermal vaporization/condensation;
- microfluidic implementations offer experimental control;
- published work already measures droplet size and pulmonary-delivery behavior;
- recent edge-effect architectures suggest the design space is still active.

Key uncertainty: **non-thermal does not mean biologically safe.** We must measure heating, material transfer, aerosol chemistry, deposited dose and biological effects rather than infer safety from mechanism.

Subfamilies to review:
- conventional SAW;
- microfluidic SAW;
- edge-effect SAW;
- disposable/contact-isolated acoustic architectures.

## Candidate 2 — Vibrating Mesh Nebulizer (VMN)

A perforated vibrating membrane drives liquid into micron-scale droplets. VMN is clinically mature and portable, making it an excellent reference for low-temperature aerosol generation.

Central question: can a mechanism optimized for pulmonary drug deposition be adapted toward high sensory/optical efficiency while **reducing**, rather than maximizing, respiratory deposition?

## Candidate 3 — Soft-Mist / mechanical microfluidics

Mechanical energy drives liquid through microstructures/nozzles to form a slow aerosol plume without conventional thermal vaporization.

Interest:
- no resistive heater required;
- portable implementations already exist in medicine;
- plume velocity and droplet distribution provide a different design space from acoustic systems.

## Candidate 4 — Electrohydrodynamic atomization / electrospray

Electric fields generate highly controllable charged droplets.

Interest:
- narrow particle-size distributions;
- potentially strong control over aerosol physics.

Major unknowns:
- achievable plume density;
- electrical charge effects;
- respiratory deposition;
- device complexity and safety.

This remains exploratory until those questions are resolved.

## Reference technologies

### Jet/pneumatic nebulization
Useful mature non-thermal reference, but less attractive for a compact recreational form factor.

### Thermal nicotine-free e-cigarette
Used only as a recreational aerosol benchmark. It provides a comparison for plume visibility, mass output, carbonyl formation, metals and other emissions. It is not the target architecture.

## Decision principle

Mechanisms do not advance because they “sound safer.” They advance when measurements show favorable performance across:

1. emitted mass;
2. aerosol size distribution;
3. optical efficiency;
4. estimated/measured respiratory deposition;
5. chemical emissions;
6. material contamination;
7. biological response.

A candidate can be terminated at any gate.

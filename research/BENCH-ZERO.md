# Bench Zero — Low-Cost, Non-Inhalation Validation

## Purpose

Test whether the core HealthPod hypotheses are worth pursuing **without using a person as the exposure detector**.

Bench Zero is designed around relative, reproducible comparisons. It does not establish inhalation safety.

## Tier 0 — almost no specialized equipment

A controlled enclosure / background and repeatable camera geometry can test:

- plume visibility;
- plume projected area;
- grayscale/optical signal over time;
- persistence half-life;
- plume trajectory;
- run-to-run reproducibility.

Record ambient temperature and relative humidity because evaporation is part of the phenomenon.

A sufficiently precise balance can estimate reservoir/device mass change across repeated standardized runs. The useful output is a normalized comparison, not a claim of pulmonary dose.

Temperature measurements can identify unexpected heating of the liquid/device.

Electrical measurements can compare energy per aerosol output.

## Tier 1 — inexpensive instrumentation

Potential additions:
- humidity/temperature logger;
- calibrated scale appropriate to expected mass changes;
- standardized light source and fixed camera exposure;
- optical particle sensing used cautiously for relative trends only;
- capture media downstream for later gravimetric or analytical work.

Consumer particle sensors are **not** substitutes for aerodynamic particle sizing or chemical analysis.

## What Bench Zero can answer

- Does SAW produce a repeatable plume?
- Which carrier class gives more optical signal per emitted mass?
- Does visible persistence change dramatically with humidity?
- Does the device heat substantially?
- Is the candidate stable enough to justify proper aerosol sizing and chemistry?

## What Bench Zero cannot answer

- whether an aerosol is safe to inhale;
- deep-lung deposited dose;
- carbonyl/VOC/metals profile without appropriate analytical methods;
- cytotoxicity or chronic toxicity;
- disease-risk reduction.

## First experiment design

Use a fixed geometry, fixed camera settings, controlled background/illumination and repeated runs. Compare candidate **physical baselines** without human inhalation. Predefine endpoints before collecting data.

Primary endpoint: relative optical signal integrated over time per measured emitted mass.

Secondary endpoints:
- plume persistence;
- apparent plume geometry;
- device/liquid temperature change;
- repeatability;
- energy consumption.

The experiment should be capable of falsifying the claim that a candidate has superior optical efficiency.

## Escalation gate

Do not optimize for sensory use until a candidate:
1. performs reproducibly on Bench Zero;
2. has credible particle-size characterization;
3. has a respiratory-deposition model;
4. has appropriate chemical/material analysis.

Human inhalation is not a Bench Zero endpoint.

# Sprint 2 — Evidence Notes

This is a living evidence map, not a claim of safety.

## SAW and liquid properties

- Huang Q-Y et al. (2026). *Viscosity-regulated acoustic streaming–thermal coupling in surface acoustic wave atomization of high-viscosity fluids.* Thermal Science and Engineering Progress. DOI: 10.1016/j.tsep.2026.104827.
  - Reports strong redistribution of SAW energy toward viscous dissipation as viscosity increases in glycerol/water systems, with atomization suppression.
- Collins DJ et al. (2012). *Atomization off thin water films generated by high-frequency substrate wave vibrations.* Physical Review E. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevE.86.056312.
  - Establishes thin aqueous-film SAW atomization and links film/power/frequency physics to droplet size.
- Roudini M et al. (2024). *Developments for SAW-based aerosol generation: Miniaturized, cost-efficient, mass-producible, and reproducible systems.* Aerosol Science and Technology. DOI: 10.1080/02786826.2024.2343377.
  - Demonstrates compact microfluidic SAW aerosol generation and controllable droplet-size regimes.

## Hygroscopic growth / deposition

- Haddrell AE et al. (2017). *Pulmonary aerosol delivery and the importance of growth dynamics.* Therapeutic Delivery. DOI: 10.4155/TDE-2017-0093.
  - Reviews how humidity-driven growth and evaporation alter aerosol disposition.
- Haddrell AE et al. (2014). *Dynamics of aerosol size during inhalation: hygroscopic growth of commercial nebulizer formulations.* International Journal of Pharmaceutics. PMID: 24406674.
  - Shows formulation-dependent water mass transfer; saline cannot automatically stand in for more complex formulations.

## PG/VG biological evidence

- Chu M et al. (2024). *Conventional and multi-omics assessments of subacute inhalation toxicity due to propylene glycol and vegetable glycerin aerosol produced by electronic cigarettes.* Ecotoxicology and Environmental Safety. DOI: 10.1016/j.ecoenv.2024.116002.
- Mittal K et al. (2026). *Propylene glycol and vegetable glycerin e-cigarette aerosols impact mucociliary function and cause cytotoxicity in human airway epithelium.* Scientific Reports. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-33023-5.
- Toxicology Letters (2025). *Toxicity of humectants propylene glycol and vegetable glycerin in electronic nicotine delivery systems.* DOI: 10.1016/j.toxlet.2025.111739.

Interpretation: PG/VG are useful incumbent comparators, not zero-risk controls. Thermal e-cigarette evidence also confounds carrier effects with thermal degradation, reinforcing the need to characterize non-thermal systems independently.
